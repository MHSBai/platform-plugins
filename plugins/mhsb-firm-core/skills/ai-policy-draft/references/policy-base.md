# AI policy base

This file is the structure that `/ai-policy-draft` fills. It is not a policy. Each section names the position the base takes, the decision the firm must make, and the reason the decision matters. The draft that results is for review by counsel licensed in each jurisdiction where the firm practices, and it has no effect until the accountable attorney adopts it with a name and a date.

No rule of any state, court or bar is stated here as settled. Where a section depends on a jurisdiction, the draft carries the line "verify with counsel" and a blank for the authority the firm's counsel supplies. Citations are never generated.

## Placeholders

Every placeholder is written in capitals in square brackets. The draft is not complete while any bracket remains. The decision sheet lists each unresolved placeholder with the person who can supply it.

## 1. Purpose and scope

Position: the policy governs every use of AI tools for firm work by attorneys, staff and contractors, on firm premises, remotely and on personal devices.

Decision: who the policy covers by role, and whether contractors sign the same acknowledgment.

## 2. Definitions

Position: "AI tools" is defined broadly. It includes chat assistants, agentic tools that take multi-step actions, notetakers and transcription tools, and AI features embedded in other software ("background AI").

Decision: the named administrator of the policy (one person, with title and contact). One named owner matters more than the title.

Defined terms the draft carries: administrator; AI tools; agentic AI tools; background AI; client confidential information; sensitive information; inputs; outputs; work product.

## 3. Core principles

3.1 Professional duties. AI use stays within the firm's professional conduct rules on competence, confidentiality, supervision of others, candor to tribunals, client communication and fees. The draft names these duties by subject; counsel supplies the rule numbers for each jurisdiction.

3.2 Client consent and engagement letters. Decision: whether the engagement letter carries an AI disclosure sentence, and its wording. The sentence is drafted separately and adopted by the attorney.

3.3 Data minimization. Position: enter only the minimum information a task needs; prefer de-identified or hypothetical facts; never enter sensitive information into a tool the firm has not approved for it.

3.4 Approved tools only. Position: firm work uses only firm-approved accounts on terms that exclude training on firm data. Decision: the approved tool list (Appendix A), and the process for requesting a new tool.

## 4. Verification of citations and facts

Position: every output is checked by a person before it goes to a supervisor, client, court or other recipient. Each citation is confirmed to exist and to say what the output says it says; each factual assertion is checked against a primary source; quotations are checked word for word.

Decision: the research platform and citator the firm uses for verification, and the consequence of a failure to verify.

## 5. Tool-specific use

5.1 Non-enterprise tools. Position: personal, free-tier and consumer accounts are not used for firm work.

5.2 Enterprise workspaces. Decision: which workspaces may hold client confidential information, and which features (file connectors, agentic or on-device use) need the administrator's approval and further training.

5.3 Background AI. Position: AI features embedded in other software may be used, and their output is reviewed like any other output. Decision: the list of such platforms (Appendix A).

5.4 Client use of AI. Position: clients are told that entering privileged communications into their own consumer AI tools may not be confidential and may affect privilege. Decision: where the firm says this (engagement letter, welcome material, or both).

## 6. Notetakers and transcription

6.1 Consent. Position: the base requires the affirmative consent of every participant before any notetaker or transcription tool runs, whether or not the law of a participant's location would permit one-party consent. Decision: whether the firm keeps this stricter standard. The draft states the trade-off if the firm relaxes it.

6.2 Approved notetakers. Decision: the one or more notetakers approved for client communications, and how transcripts are stored and retained.

6.3 Client notetakers. Position: clients are told that their own transcripts of attorney communications may not be privileged and may be discoverable.

## 7. Agentic AI

Position: an agentic tool is used only with the administrator's prior written approval. It must ask a person to confirm before any action that changes data, sends a message or spends money. A tool that cannot be configured that way is not used for such actions. Unattended runs over client data, autonomous access to sensitive information and chains of agentic tools are not permitted without a specific written exception.

Decision: who may approve an exception, and how it is recorded.

## 8. Employment decisions

Position: AI tools are not used to recruit, select, evaluate, promote, compensate or discipline personnel.

Decision: outright prohibition, or use only with the administrator's approval and a documented review. Counsel confirms the jurisdictional requirements.

## 9. Oversight and administration

9.1 Governance. Decision: the administrator alone, or a committee chaired by the administrator.

9.2 Monitoring and incidents. Position: the firm may audit use of firm accounts, including inputs and outputs; credentials are not shared; access ends with employment. Any incident (a policy violation, a security event, a material error, or a disclosure of privileged information) is reported to the administrator at once. Decision: the reporting channel.

9.3 Enforcement. Decision: which violations carry the most serious consequence. The base lists four candidates: use of an unapproved tool for firm work; failure to verify output before it is sent or filed; entry of client confidential information into an unapproved tool; use of firm tools for non-firm business. The firm aligns these with its own employment practices.

9.4 Billing. Position: time billed reflects time spent; time saved by a tool is not billed; tool subscriptions not attributable to a client are overhead. Decision: how the firm describes AI-assisted work on invoices, if at all.

9.5 Training. Position: training precedes access and covers professional duties, data security, fabricated output, jurisdiction-specific requirements and tool-specific guidance. Decision: who delivers it and how completion is recorded. `/ai-training` supplies the role lessons.

9.6 Review cadence. Decision: the interval for review of the policy (the base proposes at least once a year and on any material change in tools or rules) and who performs it.

## Acknowledgment

Every person covered signs an acknowledgment that they have read the policy, that inputs and outputs are firm records, and that violations carry the stated consequences. Decision: how acknowledgments are collected and kept.

## Appendix A: approved tools

Three lists, each filled by the firm: approved AI tools with their permitted uses; approved notetakers; background AI platforms. No vendor is named in the base.

## Appendix B: jurisdiction obligations

One entry per jurisdiction where the firm practices. Each entry is blank until counsel supplies and verifies the authority. An entry the drafter could not verify reads "verify with counsel". No entry is copied forward from another firm's policy.

## Closing lines the draft carries

The draft states that it is not legal advice, that counsel review is required before adoption, and that a policy is one part of AI governance alongside a tool inventory, training, acknowledgment records and a recurring review.
